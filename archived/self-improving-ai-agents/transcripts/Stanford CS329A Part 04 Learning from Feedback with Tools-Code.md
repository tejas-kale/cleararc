It's some form of interaction with either the environment, some form of critique mechanism, or grounding using what we know should be the right answer. Let's start with tool calling as the first abstraction. What ReAct really tried to focus on was this notion of when humans want to go and do some action, they think about it first. A similar loop can be very useful in terms of refining what language models generate. Typically, when you look at language models, they have struggled to combine reasoning and acting by themselves as isolated processes. In today's models, the reasoning has become innate because thinking has become part of the models. But if you look at the history of LLMs in the last couple of years, this was not something that came out of the box. One of the challenges of why it was hard for models to combine reasoning and acting is because they typically will hallucinate.
