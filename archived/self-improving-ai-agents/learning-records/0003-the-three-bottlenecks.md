# The course's own summary of what is unsolved: diversity, verification, task supply

Lecture 9 names three bottlenecks on the self-improvement loop — diversity collapse when one model generates its own training data, verification (including verifiers that fabricate criticism, which meta-verification exists to catch), and the human-curated supply of training prompts. All three are the same principle at different scales: **learning happens where outcomes are mixed**, and a homogeneous system stops learning.

**Implications:** this is the spine to hang future sessions on, because the practitioner versions are all cheap — use different model families rather than one model at high temperature; check whether a rejecting judge's criticism is actually true; curate eval sets toward the band that sometimes passes. Also worth holding onto for scoping arguments: the lecturers separate *slow* verification (scientific discovery, chip design, wet lab — ground truth exists but takes days) from *genuinely subjective* (creative work, taste). Most of Tejas's work is the first kind, so "we can't evaluate this" is usually a proxy-design problem, not an impossibility. See [[0001-practitioner-not-researcher]].
