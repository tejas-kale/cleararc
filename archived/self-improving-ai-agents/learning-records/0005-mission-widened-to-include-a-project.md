# The mission widened: a course-project-scale experiment is now in scope

On 2026-08-23 Tejas reversed the constraint recorded in [[0001-practitioner-not-researcher]]. After hearing what CS329A asks of its own students, he asked for research ideas that come out of the course, at the scale of a course project rather than a PhD. The mission now includes running one experiment with a control on his own workflows and writing up the result.

This does not undo the original record. He still does not want to write papers, read for a literature review, or work at the RL-internals level, and the homework and team-project structure stay out of scope. What changed is that "no research" turned out to mean "no research career", not "no experiments".

**Evidence:** he chose the CS329A course-project scale explicitly over both a weekend experiment and a workshop paper, when offered all three.

**Implications:** every lesson now carries a project hook, which is a testable question that lesson raises plus the measurement that would answer it. A new Arc V (lessons 15 to 17) covers choosing a question, designing the experiment, and writing up the result. The hooks matter more than the arc, because the failure mode here is not running the experiment badly, it is picking a question too big to finish. Lesson 15 exists mainly to force the scope down.

The lecturers' own list of open problems in Lecture 9, which is diversity collapse, verification, and the human-curated supply of task prompts, is the natural source of questions. See [[0003-the-three-bottlenecks]]. The practitioner-scale versions of all three are cheap to test, which is exactly what makes them good project material.
