# Mission: Autumn neighbourhood photographs on iPhone Air

## Why
Tejas is going to publish a month of short Berlin blog posts — neighbourhood autumn photographs made on an iPhone Air, edited only in Photos — not a gear essay and not a destination-park portfolio. The skill that has to exist first is making honest, publishable frames with the camera he actually has, so the posts can be specific about what the files support.

## Success looks like
- Can name, without looking it up, what the Air has (one 48MP Fusion main, 26 mm ƒ/1.6, optical-quality 2×) and what it does not (ultra-wide, dedicated telephoto, macro, ProRAW).
- Can set 24MP HEIF as the default, and say when 48MP is worth turning on.
- Can protect yellow foliage from clipping, then finish a keeper in Photos in two minutes.
- Can walk a local loop and come back with a named composition, not a pavement document.
- Can make a two-scale pair (place + one belonging detail) without a macro lens.
- Can cull a month to 6–8 images and write 400–800 words that a stranger could read as a place and a season.

## Constraints
- Stock Camera + Photos only. No Halide, no Lightroom colour mixer, no clip-on glass.
- Two-minute edit budget per keeper. No fake autumn colour, no sky replacement, no Clean Up as a creative tool.
- Weekday walks stay local. Peak colour is usually mid to late October; later lessons should land in that window if possible.
- Blog JPEGs ≤ ~2500 px on the long edge. Default capture is 24MP HEIF.
- Posts should read like the rest of the blog: specific, a bit sceptical of defaults, clear about what the files will and will not support.

## Out of scope
- Pro-model technique (ultra-wide, tetraprism telephoto, macro, ProRAW, Apple Log).
- Video, Dual Capture, and the 18MP front camera.
- Destination shoots as the syllabus (Tiergarten, Grunewald, Botanischer Garten are optional weekends, not the course).
- Building a photography career or a general iPhone Camera encyclopaedia.
