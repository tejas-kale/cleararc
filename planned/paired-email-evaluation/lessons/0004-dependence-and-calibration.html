Lesson 4 of 5 · Paired email evaluation

Count email units, not decisions

Three predictions on one email are useful paired evidence, not three independent emails. The same discipline limits calibration claims.

≈7 minutes · Dependence and probability calibration

More model outputs are not more sampled emails

Three models each classify the same 100 emails, producing 300 decisions. Those 300 decisions share the same 100 email contents, labels, difficulty, senders, and topic mix. They are correlated by design. For each model's accuracy, the sample size is still 100; for an A-versus-B comparison, there are still 100 paired email records.

Related emails introduce another layer. Ten messages from one thread or a campaign are not ten fully fresh draws from the inbox. If you retain such clusters, treat the thread or cluster as the resampling unit in the paired bootstrap, or deduplicate before sampling. Otherwise the nominal n overstates the diversity of evidence.

Check yourself

Three models classify one ambiguous email. How many independent email units has that created for estimating each model's accuracy?

Correct. Ambiguity may be important to document, but it does not multiply the number of independent sampled emails.

Probability quality is not calibration

A classifier may give probabilities, not just labels. Probability quality asks whether assigned probabilities receive good scoring-rule values across predictions; Brier score and log loss can be reported per prediction. Calibration makes the stronger frequency claim: among events assigned about 70% probability, did roughly 70% happen?

With 100 observations split into five confidence bins, an equal-mass plot gives about 20 observations per bin. A class-specific view has fewer. If 14 of 20 outcomes occur in a bin, the displayed rate is 70%, but one outcome changes it by 5 points; a few outcomes change its apparent slope substantially. Equal-width bins can be worse, leaving sparse tail bins.

A coarse equal-mass reliability view can still be a useful descriptive diagnostic. It cannot establish strong target-domain calibration from 100 emails. Do not pool different labels or decision types just to make the bins look full: that changes the event whose calibration you claim.

Retrieval practice

Why does 100 emails not become 300 independent observations merely because three models score all of them?

Each trio of predictions is driven by the same email and label. The outcomes are useful to compare models within that email, but they share difficulty and composition. Independence is about independently sampled units, not the number of values written to a table.
