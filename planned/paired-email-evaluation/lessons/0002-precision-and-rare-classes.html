Why 200 changes the picture

More emails reduce ordinary uncertainty, but not in the intuitive “twice as many means twice as precise” way.

≈8 minutes · 1/sqrt(n) and rare classes

Hold accuracy fixed at 80%

Repeat the Wilson calculation at the same observed rate. The notebook gives:

The rough scale is 1 / sqrt(n). Double 100 to 200 and the standard error becomes 1 / sqrt(2) ≈ 0.707 of its former size, not one half. To halve uncertainty, you need about four times as many independent emails: 400 rather than 100.

Check yourself

If a 100-email accuracy interval has a rough half-width of 8 points, what is the best rough expectation at 200 emails?

Correct. The actual Wilson figures are 7.8 and 5.5 points, close to that square-root rule. The rule is a scaling intuition, not a substitute for computing the interval.

The rare class is a separate problem

Overall accuracy pools every label. If an important class occurs in 5% of naturally sampled emails, a 100-email sample contains 100 × 0.05 = about 5 such emails on average. Random sampling can deliver fewer or more.

With only five examples, class-specific accuracy moves in jumps of 1 / 5 = 20 percentage points. Four correct out of five is 80%; three correct out of five is 60%. One changed email is not a subtle revision.

Even 300 emails does not make a 5% class a large-sample problem. It gives roughly 15 cases. Report it as a small, valuable case analysis; do not let a smooth-looking percentage disguise the denominator.

A deliberately balanced sample can improve evidence about a rare class, but it no longer estimates overall performance on the natural inbox mix without appropriate weighting. Decide which question you are answering.

What 200 buys you

At a common 80% outcome, 200 changes the illustrative single-model interval from ±7.8 to ±5.5 points. At a 5% class, it moves from about 5 to about 10 examples. That is a meaningful improvement, not a cure for rare outcomes.

Retrieval practice

A class appears 5 times in 100 emails. Why is “the class-specific accuracy was 80%” an incomplete report?

The denominator is only five. 80% means four correct, and one different result would change it by 20 points. The report should show the count (4/5), acknowledge the wide uncertainty, and avoid treating it as a stable rate.
