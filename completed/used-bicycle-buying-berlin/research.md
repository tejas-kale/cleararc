# Buying a used bicycle at a Berlin bicycle flea market

**Use case:** everyday Berlin riding, occasional Tegel forest paths, and carrying a toddler in a rear child seat.

**Evidence checked:** 6 September 2026. Prices below are indicative Berlin asking prices observed in current first-party listings, not a statistical transaction average.

## Short recommendation

Look first for a sound, correctly sized **trekking bike** or slightly upright hybrid with a rigid fork (or demonstrably serviceable suspension), 28-inch wheels with reasonably wide puncture-resistant tyres, reliable brakes, full mudguards, approved lights and reflectors, a bell, stable stand, strong rear rack, and low enough gearing for ramps, wind and forest inclines.

This is a better compromise than a pure city bike for the stated use. ADFC describes trekking bikes as versatile, normally equipped with lights, mudguards and a rack, and suitable for everyday use and unpaved paths ([ADFC: Trekkingräder](https://www.adfc.de/artikel/trekkingraeder)). City bikes are comfortable and practical for short urban trips, but their upright geometry is less efficient for longer rides ([ADFC: Cityräder](https://www.adfc.de/artikel/cityraeder)).

Do not buy around the child seat before checking the complete system: bicycle, rack, seat, rack mounts, wheel guards and total weight limit. The adult must be able to control the heavier, higher-centre-of-mass bicycle confidently.

## Fit and intended riding

Do not buy by frame-size label alone. Ride several bikes and adjust saddle and handlebar. At the correct saddle height, the knee should retain a slight bend at the bottom of the pedal stroke; you should reach the brake levers without stretching and stop/start confidently. ADFC’s guidance says correct adjustment aligns the working angles of legs, knees and arms ([ADFC: choosing a saddle](https://www.adfc.de/artikel/den-passenden-fahrradsattel-finden)).

For a rough first filter, one Berlin shop gives S 150–165 cm, M 165–175 cm and L 175–190 cm, but geometry varies by model ([Urby Bike size guide](https://urby.bike/de/rental/)). Treat those as search filters, not a substitute for riding the bike.

For Tegel forest, favour a stable rack, mudguards, useful low gears, straight-running wheels and grippy tyres. A trekking bike is mainly for paved roads but is also suitable for unpaved paths ([ADFC: Trekkingräder](https://www.adfc.de/artikel/trekkingraeder)); this does not make it a mountain bike, so avoid deep sand, roots and rough trails with a child seat. ADFC recommends puncture-resistant tyres, reliable brakes, a stable stand, mudguards, a rack and StVZO-compliant equipment for everyday bikes ([ADFC: everyday-bike decision aid](https://www.adfc.de/artikel/entscheidungshilfe-fuer-den-fahrradkauf)).

## Toddler child-seat compatibility

German law permits a person aged at least 16 to carry children under seven on a bicycle built and equipped for carrying people, if a special child seat and protection against feet entering the spokes are provided ([StVO §21(3)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvo_2013/__21.html)). In practice, use an approved seat with a backrest, harness, footrests and spoke guards; the child should wear a helmet.

Check the exact seat manual. For example, Thule’s rack-mounted systems require an ISO 11243-compatible rack with a minimum 27 kg load rating; width and tube diameter also matter ([Thule compatibility guide](https://www.thule.com/de-de/articles/guides/choosing-the-right-child-bike-seat-mounting-options-and-bike-compatibility)). Thule’s instructions specify seats generally from 9–22 kg and say the child must sit independently and hold their head up; consult a paediatrician before carrying a child under one year ([Thule safety instructions](https://www.thule.com/-/s/approved/std.lang.all/43/05/564305.pdf?rev=7.5)).

Before paying:

1. Identify the exact seat model and download its current manual.
2. Confirm rack standard, width, tube diameter, load rating and compatibility.
3. Add child, seat and luggage weights; stay within both rack and bicycle system limits.
4. Check that the seat will not obstruct lights, saddle, brakes, cables, panniers or the wheel.
5. Prefer a new seat if the used seat’s history is unknown. Never use one with cracks, deformation, white stress marks, damaged harnesses, missing fasteners or missing instructions; ADAC gives these as material checks for used child seats ([ADAC: inspecting used child seats](https://www.adac.de/rund-ums-fahrzeug/ausstattung-technik-zubehoer/kindersitze/kindersitzberater/kindersitze-wie-alt/)).

Test the empty bicycle first. After secure installation, test at walking speed in a quiet area with a helmeted child and another adult nearby. Never make the flea-market ride the child’s first test.

## Flea-market inspection checklist

### Walk away for structural uncertainty

- Frame/fork: no cracks, dents near joints, buckling, severe corrosion, fresh paint hiding damage, or altered serial number.
- Headset: hold the front brake and rock the bike; a knock suggests play. Turn the bars slowly; roughness or notchiness is a concern.
- Wheels: lift and spin each one; check wobble, loose spokes, damaged rims and tyre bulges.
- Hubs/bottom bracket: check for side play and grinding.
- Seatpost, stem, bars and rack: inspect clamps, rack stays and mounting points for cracks, stripped threads or movement.
- Treat impact-damaged carbon as a specialist inspection job.

### Brakes, gears and consumables

- Squeeze both brakes hard while stationary. Levers should feel firm, not pull to the bar; pads should contact evenly.
- Shift through every gear under light load. It should not skip, grind or refuse to change.
- Inspect tyres for cracking, flat spots, embedded glass and damage. Budget for replacement if age is unknown.
- Test stand, bell, grips, pedals and quick releases.

### Road legality and lighting

StVZO §67 requires approved front and rear lights, reflectors, and prescribed pedal and side reflectors; lights must be fitted and ready for use, with removable lights fitted when conditions require them ([StVZO §67](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stvzo_2012/__67.html)). Check these before treating a bike as ready for commuting.

## Test ride

Ride for at least five minutes on a quiet route: start, stop and emergency-brake; use every gear; check it tracks straight without vibration or pulling; listen for clicks, creaks, grinding and chain slap; test the stand and steering lock-to-lock; and check nothing shifts under braking or climbing. ADAC likewise recommends a thorough test ride covering brakes, gears, lights and roadworthiness ([ADAC: buying a used bicycle](https://www.adac.de/rund-ums-fahrzeug/zweirad/fahrrad-ebike-pedelec/kauf-ausruestung/gebrauchtes-fahrrad-kaufen/)).

## Theft and legal checks

Treat provenance as a purchase condition. Berlin Police advises buyers to have ownership explained, see the original purchase receipt where possible, see the seller’s ID, and use a written contract ([Berlin Police: preventing bicycle theft](https://www.berlin.de/polizei/aufgaben/praevention/diebstahl-und-einbruch/artikel.228483.php)). National police guidance likewise says to see the receipt, record buyer/seller and bicycle details in a written contract, check ID and obtain coding papers where applicable ([Polizei-Beratung: buying used bicycles](https://www.polizei-beratung.de/fileadmin/Medien/025-FB-Raeder-richtig-sichern.pdf)).

Record the frame number, make, model, colour, components and photographs. Compare the number with the receipt and coding document. Do not buy if the number is ground off, altered, absent without a convincing explanation, or the seller refuses identification or a contract. A very low price, rushed cash-only sale, inconsistent story, multiple identical bikes, or a locked/immovable display bike are red flags.

You can search Berlin Police’s page of recovered bicycles if a bike appears suspicious ([Berlin Police: missing bicycles and recovered property](https://www.berlin.de/polizei/service/vermissen-sie-ihr-fahrrad/)). This is not a guarantee of clean provenance, so retain the contract and evidence of payment.

The Berliner Fahrradmarkt provides a used-bike purchase-contract form and says its market includes dealers and private sellers ([Berlin official market page](https://www.berlin.de/special/shopping/flohmaerkte/4409904-1724959-fahrradmarkt-berlin.html); [market contract PDF](https://berlinerfahrradmarkt.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/130320_BFM_KV.pdf)).

For this use, a documented, well-maintained €300–500 trekking/hybrid bike is usually more rational than a cheap worn bike needing tyres, chain, brake work, rack replacement and lights. ADFC notes that regularly used trekking bikes below €500 often involve sizeable quality compromises ([ADFC: Trekkingräder](https://www.adfc.de/artikel/trekkingraeder)).

## Negotiation, payment and decision rule

Inspect first, then make one calm offer based on specific costs: “€X because the rear tyre and brake pads need replacement and the light is missing.” Do not negotiate away structural, braking, provenance or child-seat compatibility problems—walk away. Before paying, agree which accessories are included, complete the written contract, photograph the frame number, and use traceable payment where possible. Leave with the bicycle, keys, receipt/contract, coding papers and manuals.

Buy only if all five answers are yes:

1. Is the frame/fork structurally sound and the frame number intact?
2. Does it fit and handle safely?
3. Do brakes, gears, wheels and lights work—or are repairs fully costed?
4. Can the exact child-seat system be mounted within its ratings?
5. Has the seller proved ownership and accepted a written contract?

If any answer is no, keep your money.
