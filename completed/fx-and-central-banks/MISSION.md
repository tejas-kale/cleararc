# Mission: Foreign exchange, central banks and the machinery behind them

## Why
Tejas wants to read the finance section of *The Economist* / *FT* and follow the **mechanics** rather than skim the vibes — to reach the end of a piece like ["When Japan buys yen, it unwinds a dangerous trade"](../../Documents/Cowork%20Root/00_Resources/Clippings/When%20Japan%20buys%20yen,%20it%20unwinds%20a%20dangerous%20trade.md) and know exactly what happened on whose balance sheet. He studied economics, but roughly ten years ago; the goal is to reactivate that base and attach today's plumbing vocabulary to it. Not a career pivot into macro — fluency as a reader.

## Success looks like
- Can say what an exchange rate quote *is* (a price, a ratio, with a direction) and name the two or three forces that move it over a week versus a decade.
- Can explain where a country's foreign-exchange reserves come from, and why a central bank can sell its own currency indefinitely but can only buy it until the reserves run out.
- Can read "the BoJ's interventions are typically *sterilised*" and narrate the two offsetting transactions without looking anything up.
- Can explain why a government bond is a liability, why the central bank holding it changes the *consolidated* public-sector position, and what that consolidated sector is long and short.
- Can state, in one sentence, why rising Japanese interest rates are a problem for the Japanese government specifically.
- Re-reads the Economist article and finds no sentence opaque.

## Constraints
- **~30 minutes for the first arc.** Four short lessons, roughly seven minutes each. Lessons must be completable in one sitting, not aspirational.
- Informed-layperson baseline: comfortable with "interest rate", "inflation", "bond" in the loose newspaper sense; not comfortable with balance-sheet mechanics, sterilisation, or carry.
- Depth is capped at "enough to read confidently". Where a rabbit hole opens (UIP puzzle, optimal reserve levels), name it and move on.

## Out of scope
- Trading, position-taking, or any how-to on actually running an FX or carry position.
- Formal open-economy macro models (Mundell–Fleming, Dornbusch overshooting) beyond a passing mention.
- Japanese fiscal politics and demographics, except where the article leans on them.
- Crypto, stablecoins, and monetary-reform arguments.
