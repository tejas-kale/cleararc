# Foreign Exchange & Central Banks Glossary

The canonical language for this workspace. Where several words exist for one idea, one is chosen and the rest listed as aliases to avoid. The printable version is [reference/glossary.html](reference/glossary.html).

> **Status note (2026-08-20):** these terms were introduced in the first arc but have not yet been demonstrated in use. Per the workspace rules a term is only fully promoted once Tejas uses it correctly unprompted. Treat this file as provisional until the first post-arc conversation confirms which ones stuck.

## The price

**Exchange rate**:
The price of one currency expressed in another. Always a ratio with a direction — "¥163 to the dollar" prices the dollar, in yen.
_Avoid_: the value of the yen, currency level

**Weak / strong currency**:
Weak means each unit buys less foreign currency. On a yen-per-dollar quote, a higher number means a weaker yen.
_Avoid_: the yen is up/down

**Funding currency**:
The cheap currency a carry trade borrows in.
_Avoid_: base currency

**Uncovered interest parity (UIP)**:
The proposition that an interest-rate advantage in one currency is exactly offset by that currency's expected depreciation. Fails persistently in the data.
_Avoid_: interest rate parity — that is the covered, hedged version, which does hold

## The reserves

**Foreign-exchange reserves**:
Foreign-currency assets held by a country's monetary authorities, mostly foreign government bonds. An investment portfolio denominated in someone else's money.
_Avoid_: war chest, currency stockpile

**Intervention**:
Trading reserves against one's own currency in the open market to move the price rather than to earn a return.
_Avoid_: propping up the currency

**The asymmetry**:
A state can weaken its currency without limit because it issues what it sells; it can strengthen it only until reserves run out.
_Avoid_: no standard term exists — this workspace uses "the asymmetry"

**FEFSA**:
Japan's Foreign Exchange Fund Special Account. A government account holding the foreign-exchange reserves; the Bank of Japan spends from it only as the finance ministry's agent.
_Avoid_: the BoJ's reserves

**Exchange Stabilization Fund (ESF)**:
The US Treasury's equivalent, deployable at the Treasury Secretary's direction and executed by the New York Fed.

## The balance sheet

**Government bond**:
A dated IOU issued by the state. An asset to its holder, a liability to its issuer, with a cost fixed at issue until maturity.
_Avoid_: gilt, JGB, Treasury — use local names only when the country matters

**Yield**:
The return earned buying a bond at today's price and holding it to maturity. Moves inversely to price.
_Avoid_: interest rate, once the bond is issued

**Bank reserves**:
Balances commercial banks hold at the central bank. A central-bank liability, payable on demand and paying the policy rate — effectively overnight floating-rate debt. In Japan, "current account balances".
_Avoid_: reserves, unqualified

**Consolidated public sector**:
Treasury and central bank — and for Japan the public pension funds — viewed as one entity, with everything they owe each other netted out.
_Avoid_: the government, when the central bank is included

**Rolling over**:
Reissuing a maturing bond at today's interest rate.
_Avoid_: refinancing

## The trade

**Carry trade**:
Borrowing in a low-yielding currency to hold higher-yielding assets in another, without hedging the currency risk.
_Avoid_: currency arbitrage — arbitrage is riskless, this is not

**Carry**:
The interest-rate gap between what a position earns and what it costs to fund.
_Avoid_: spread, yield pickup

**Sterilised intervention**:
An intervention paired with an offsetting open-market operation, so the currency trade happens without changing the money supply or the policy rate.
_Avoid_: neutralised intervention

**Unwinding**:
Closing a position — here, shrinking both sides of the state's carry trade at once.
_Avoid_: deleveraging, when only one side is moving

## Ambiguity resolved

In this workspace, unqualified **"reserves" always means foreign-exchange reserves** — a state's assets. The liability kind is always written in full as **"bank reserves"**. The article uses both senses within four paragraphs of each other, and this is the single most common place to lose the thread.
