# Foreign Exchange & Central Banks — Resources

Curated, high-trust sources. Lessons cite from here rather than from memory.

## Knowledge

### Primary institutional sources (highest trust — these are the operators themselves)

- [Bank of Japan — *Outline of the Bank of Japan's Foreign Exchange Intervention Operations*](https://www.boj.or.jp/en/intl_finance/outline/expkainyu.htm)
  The operator's own description of the plumbing: the Foreign Exchange Fund Special Account (FEFSA), how yen-selling intervention is funded by issuing Financing Bills, and how yen-buying spends existing dollar holdings. **Use for:** anything about how a Japanese intervention is actually financed. This is the source that settles arguments.
- [Bank of Japan — *What is foreign exchange intervention? Who decides and conducts it?*](https://www.boj.or.jp/en/about/education/oshiete/intl/g19.htm)
  Short, plain-language. Establishes the division of labour: the **Minister of Finance decides**, the **BoJ executes as agent**. **Use for:** correcting the newspaper shorthand "the BoJ intervened".
- [Ministry of Finance Japan — Foreign exchange intervention operations data](https://www.mof.go.jp/english/policy/international_policy/reference/feint/index.htm)
  Monthly and daily published intervention amounts. **Use for:** checking whether a reported intervention actually happened and at what size, instead of relying on press estimates.
- [Federal Reserve Bank of New York — *U.S. Foreign Exchange Intervention* (Fedpoints)](https://www.newyorkfed.org/aboutthefed/fedpoint/fed44.html)
  Explains that the New York Fed executes FX operations both for the Fed's own account (SOMA, at FOMC direction) and for the **Treasury's Exchange Stabilization Fund** (at the Treasury Secretary's direction), and that it sometimes acts as agent for foreign central banks. **Use for:** understanding what Scott Bessent was actually reaching into, and the US side of a joint intervention.
- [U.S. Treasury — Exchange Stabilization Fund](https://home.treasury.gov/policy-issues/international/exchange-stabilization-fund)
  The fund the Treasury Secretary can deploy without new appropriations. **Use for:** who in America can move FX reserves, and on whose say-so.

### Research

- [Chien, Cole & Lustig — *What About Japan?* (NBER Working Paper 31850)](https://www.nber.org/papers/w31850)
  The paper the article is built on. Argues Japan's consolidated public sector runs a global currency carry trade: borrowing short in yen, holding a large unhedged stock of risky foreign assets, earning a return **above 6% of GDP** over its funding cost across the last decade. **Use for:** the claim that the Japanese state *is* the world's largest carry trader, and for the numbers behind it. [Free PDF](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w31850/w31850.pdf).
- [Hanno Lustig — *The Two Cents*: "What about Japan?" and "The carry trade that can't be unwound"](https://thetwocents.substack.com/p/the-carry-trade-that-cant-be-unwound)
  One of the paper's authors explaining the argument in blog form, with the rate-rise problem spelled out. **Use for:** the intuition, when the paper is too heavy. Author-written, so it is a primary account of the argument even though it is a blog.
- [BIS — *Sizing up carry trades in BIS statistics* (Quarterly Review, Sept 2024)](https://www.bis.org/publ/qtrpdf/r_qt2409y.htm)
  Measures how big yen-funded carry positions actually are, and what data can and cannot see. **Use for:** scale, and for healthy scepticism about any single carry-trade estimate.
- [San Francisco Fed — *Interest Rates, Carry Trades, and Exchange Rate Movements* (Economic Letter)](https://www.frbsf.org/research-and-insights/publications/economic-letter/2006/11/interest-rates-carry-trades-and-exchange-rate-movements/)
  Compact explanation of uncovered interest parity, why it fails empirically, and why that failure is the source of carry profit. **Use for:** the "why does this trade make money at all?" question.
- [Federal Reserve Board — *An Assessment of the Impact of Japanese Foreign Exchange Intervention: 1991–2004* (IFDP 824)](https://www.federalreserve.gov/pubs/ifdp/2005/824/ifdp824.htm)
  Empirical study of whether Japanese intervention moved the yen. **Use for:** the honest answer to "does intervention even work?" — a genuinely contested question.
- [Richmond Fed — Hetzel, *Sterilized Foreign Exchange Intervention: The Fed Debate in the 1960s*](https://www.richmondfed.org/-/media/richmondfedorg/publications/research/economic_quarterly/1996/spring/pdf/hetzel.pdf)
  History of the argument over whether sterilised intervention can do anything at all. **Use for:** depth on sterilisation once the mechanics are solid.
- [Minneapolis Fed — *Sterilized FX: all show and no dough?*](https://www.minneapolisfed.org/article/2001/sterilized-fx-they-may-attract-attention-and-make-people-feel-good-but-ultimately-theyre-all-show-and-no-dough)
  The sceptical case, plainly argued. **Use for:** hearing the other side before forming a view.

### Reference / data

- [BIS Triennial Central Bank Survey of FX and OTC derivatives markets](https://www.bis.org/statistics/rpfx22.htm)
  The authoritative measure of FX market size and currency shares. **Use for:** any "how big is the FX market" claim.
- [IMF COFER — Currency Composition of Official Foreign Exchange Reserves](https://data.imf.org/en/datasets/IMF.STA:COFER)
  What the world's reserves are actually held in. **Use for:** dollar-share-of-reserves claims.

## Wisdom (Communities)

- [r/AskEconomics](https://www.reddit.com/r/AskEconomics/)
  Heavily moderated; answers are restricted to approved contributors with economics training, which makes it unusually high-signal for a subreddit. **Use for:** "is my understanding of this mechanism right?" questions — exactly the kind this mission generates.
- [Marginal Revolution](https://marginalrevolution.com/) comment threads and the [Money Stuff](https://www.bloomberg.com/account/newsletters/money-stuff) readership
  Practitioner-heavy, opinionated. **Use for:** seeing how people who trade this stuff talk about it, which is different from how it is taught.
- [Central Banking Forum / CFA Society local chapters](https://www.cfainstitute.org/community/societies)
  Real-world, in-person, budget permitting. **Use for:** wisdom that only comes from people who do it for a living.

## Gaps

- **No good single explainer of a central bank balance sheet** aimed at an informed layperson has been found yet. The Bank of England's "Money creation in the modern economy" is adjacent but not the same thing. Worth hunting before the next arc.
- **Nothing yet on the 1992 sterling crisis** from a primary or near-primary source; the article opens with it and it is the perfect mirror-image case study (a central bank buying its own currency and losing).
- **No source yet on how reserve *adequacy* is judged** (IMF ARA metric or similar), which is what would let us answer "how long could Japan keep doing this?".
