# Prior knowledge: economics background, ten years cold; hard 30-minute budget

Tejas holds a background in economics but last studied it around 2016, and describes his current level as "informed layperson". He is comfortable with the newspaper senses of interest rates, inflation and bonds, and does not need supply-and-demand or "what is a bond" taught from zero — but balance-sheet mechanics, sterilisation and carry were explicitly unfamiliar.

He capped the first session at **30 minutes**, which set the shape of the whole arc: four lessons of roughly seven minutes rather than two deeper ones.

**Why it matters for future sessions:** the floor is higher than a true beginner's but the plumbing is genuinely new, so the productive move is always "one line of refresher, then straight to the mechanism". And the time cap appears to be a real constraint rather than a preference — assume future sessions are similarly bounded and size lessons accordingly.

**Implications:** depth on the theoretical rabbit holes (UIP as a puzzle, optimal reserve levels, Mundell–Fleming) was deliberately deferred rather than skipped. Those are available as second-arc material if he asks, and [[NOTES.md]] lists them. See [[MISSION.md]].
