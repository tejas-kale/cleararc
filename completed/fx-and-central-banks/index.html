Foreign exchange & central banks

Four short lessons that take you from "what is ¥163 to the dollar" to reading The Economist's "When Japan buys yen, it unwinds a dangerous trade" without a single opaque sentence.

≈30 minutes end to end · Started 20 August 2026

The arc

Each lesson unlocks a specific part of the article, and each builds on the one before. Do them in order; the fourth depends on the third rather heavily.

#LessonWhat it unlocksTime
1 The exchange rate is a price What ¥163 means, which direction is "weak", and who sets the price 7 min
2 Reserves, and what it means to intervene The asymmetry, who really spends the money, and the ¥5.7trn profit 7 min
3 Bonds, and the state as one balance sheet Why QE shortened Japan's debt instead of shrinking it 8 min
4 The carry trade, and why buying yen unwinds it Carry, sterilisation, and the article's actual argument 8 min

Reference — the part you keep

  • Glossary — the canonical vocabulary, with the aliases to avoid. Settles the two-meanings-of-"reserves" problem.
  • One-page cheat sheet — six rules that always hold, plus the four questions that decode any intervention story. Prints to one page.
  • The article, decoded — the original walked through sentence by sentence.

The workspace

Lessons are the teaching; these files are the state.

  • MISSION.md — why this topic, and what success looks like. Everything traces back here.
  • RESOURCES.md — the trusted sources lessons cite from, plus an explicit list of gaps.
  • GLOSSARY.md — the glossary in source form.
  • learning-records/ — what has actually been learned, which decides what comes next.
  • NOTES.md — preferences and teaching notes.
  • assets/ — the shared stylesheet and quiz components every lesson is built from.
How to use this

Do the lessons in one sitting if you can — they were sized for it. Answer the quiz and retrieval prompts before revealing; the effort of failing to remember is what builds retention, and reading the answer straight away feels productive while doing almost nothing. Then bring your questions back to your teacher, especially the ones that start "but surely…".
